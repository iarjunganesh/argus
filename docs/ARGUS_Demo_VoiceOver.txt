Know Your Customer — or KYC — is one of the most expensive and time-consuming tasks in financial compliance.

Before a bank can onboard a new customer, it must verify identity, check sanctions lists, screen for adverse media, resolve corporate ownership, and assess regulatory risk.

Doing this manually takes days. And the cost of getting it wrong can be enormous.

This is ARGUS.

ARGUS — Agentic Risk and Governance Unified Screening — is a multi-agent KYC system built on Azure AI Foundry, created for the Microsoft Agents League Hackathon 2026.

A single KYC request is broken into four specialist agents running in parallel — Identity, Screening, Corporate Intelligence, and Transaction Intelligence. Their results are then passed to the Compliance and Risk Agent as a fan-in step, all coordinated by an Orchestrator using the Agent-to-Agent protocol.

The knowledge retrieval layer is powered by Foundry IQ, with three knowledge bases — one for regulatory text, one for sanctions data, and one for adverse media. The adverse-media corpus combines synthetic scenarios with public-source summaries. Every answer is cited, grounded, and traceable.

All customer and transactional records in this demonstration are synthetic. Public-source adverse-media summaries are used for realistic screening context.

Let me show you ARGUS in action.

This is the ARGUS demo interface, built with Gradio and FastAPI.

First, I submit a KYC request for Cayman Synth Capital — a synthetic corporate entity registered in the Cayman Islands, a high-risk jurisdiction under FATF guidelines.

I enter the entity name, select corporate, and set the jurisdiction to KY.

I click Submit.

Four agents fire in parallel immediately — Identity, Screening, Corporate Intelligence, and Transaction. The Orchestrator coordinates all four simultaneously.

Within seconds, all results return. The Orchestrator passes everything to the Compliance and Risk Agent — the fan-in step — which synthesises the final risk score.

The report is ready.

The report opens with the ARGUS DECISION card — an executive summary showing the risk tier, numeric score, confidence percentage, recommendation, and primary drivers behind the decision. Below it, the OCR Visibility strip shows the three-step document flow: Upload, Extract, and Investigate.

Below the executive card, ARGUS shows the full risk dimension breakdown.

In the Regulatory Triggers section, every regulatory reference includes a Foundry IQ citation — the knowledge base name, the source document, and the article number.

FATF Recommendation 12, cited from the FATF 40 Recommendations. FATF Recommendation 19, for high-risk third countries.

A compliance decision without a traceable source has no legal standing. ARGUS solves this by design, using Foundry IQ as the intelligence layer.

The Investigation Timeline shows when each agent completed and the total latency for the full assessment.

The overall risk tier for Cayman Synth Capital is shown at the top, together with the score for this run.

Key findings include adverse media detection, a multi-level ownership chain with a high-risk jurisdiction node, and a transaction structuring pattern that matches a FATF smurfing typology.

The recommended actions are clear. Enhanced Due Diligence is required. A source-of-funds declaration must be obtained. The case must be escalated to a Senior Compliance Officer. And a Suspicious Activity Report should be considered.

In Regulatory Triggers and findings, citations remain explicit and auditable — knowledge base name, source document, and snippet metadata.

This contrast is the point. The first case shows a high-risk synthetic typology path. The second case shows a public-source intelligence path. Same orchestration. Same controls. Different evidence patterns.

The audit trace confirms all five agents were invoked, and reports tool-call and Foundry IQ query counts with full traceability.

ARGUS demonstrates how multi-agent reasoning, the Agent-to-Agent protocol, and Foundry IQ can transform a complex, multi-day compliance workflow into a fast, cited, and fully auditable process — in seconds.
